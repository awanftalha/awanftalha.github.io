## /* Four jobs, start to finish */

1. **Get in.** Email, a 6-digit code, a passkey, done. No password.
2. **Send.** Pick a person, see the amount in dollars and crypto, confirm with Face ID, watch it arrive.
3. **Trade with people.** Buy or sell USDT for PKR with the crypto locked in escrow until payment is confirmed.
4. **Stay safe.** Know every device, cap what can leave, freeze in one tap, recover with people you trust.

## /* Key moments */

<Decision n="01" title="Catch the fake address before it costs you">
  <Loop slot="media" poster="/assets/work/coincase/fake-1.webp" alt="Send: This looks like a fake address, with Daniyal's saved address and the one just pasted side by side, the different middle highlighted." device="phone" />

Scammers send tiny payments from addresses that start and end like a friend's, hoping you copy one from your history. When you paste one, CoinCase stops you and shows both addresses side by side with the different middle highlighted. In Activity, those zero-value payments are folded away as "2 hidden transfers" instead of sitting next to real ones.
</Decision>

<Decision n="02" title="A payment that tells you where it is">
  <Loop slot="media" poster="/assets/work/coincase/send-tracker.webp" alt="On its way to Daniyal: 8 of 12 network checks done, about 20 seconds left." device="phone" />

The confirm sheet says exactly what will happen ("this only sends 0.07832 ETH, no other changes to your wallet") and that it can't be reversed. Then a live tracker replaces "pending": checks done, time left, and a way to speed it up if it stalls. Home keeps the same tracker in its "In motion" card, and the payment ends at "Daniyal has your 0.07832 ETH".
</Decision>

<Decision n="03" title="Trade with a stranger, safely">
  <Loop slot="media" poster="/assets/work/coincase/p2p-seller.webp" alt="The seller's screen: Hamza says he has paid, check your bank app before you release the USDT." device="phone" />

The buyer sees the USDT locked in escrow before paying, with the exact Raast ID, amount and reference, and a warning never to write "crypto" in the bank note. The seller gets the hardest moment designed for them: check your bank app, not the SMS; the name must match; "nobody is rushing you." If the seller goes quiet, the buyer opens an appeal and the USDT stays locked until a specialist decides.
</Decision>

## /* One product, three brands */

Same screens, swapped brand tokens. Every brand colour lives in the Brand variable collection. A bank switches the mode, uploads a logo and name, and the whole app re-skins with no layout changes. Halden Bank and Saffra are fictional examples.

## /* Where it stands */

Shipped on iOS, with a responsive web app covering the same flows on desktop and in a phone browser. The IPO failed and the product was shut down.

Every flow includes its failure states: not enough balance, failed payment, expired quote, expired P2P order, ID photo too dark, frozen account.
