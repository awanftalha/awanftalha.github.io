SnapGrid · Web and mobile app

From finished job to first post

A web app for the office and a phone app for the crew, built for home-service companies. Ask customers for photos the moment a job is done, reward the ones you approve, and publish them with consent attached.

Role

Lead product designer

Product

SnapGrid web and mobile app

Platform

Web and iOS

People

Owner, crew, homeowner

Problem

The best proof of a finished job lived in customers’ camera rolls and crews’ phones, with no consent and no route to the website.

My role

Lead product designer. The office web app, the crew’s iOS app and the design system they share.

What changed

One loop from the job site to the feed: capture, ask, review, reward, publish. Each step works on the screen where it happens.

Result

A web app and an iOS app on one design system, with the failure states designed as carefully as the happy path.

Maria’s kitchen, install to Instagram

2:14 PM Capture Jordan shoots the island before he packs up. Before and after line up.
2:20 PM Ask One tap texts Maria her upload link, $25 reward included.
7:02 PM Upload Four photos from Maria’s couch. No app, no account.
7:15 PM Review Three approved, one back as blurry, with a reason she’ll see.
7:16 PM Reward $25 joins the queue. Seven rewards go out in one send.
SUN 10:00 Publish The best shot becomes a post. Consent is already on file.

The rest of the loop

When it fails, nothing is lost

Most of the screens in this project are not the happy path. Every failure has a cause, a reassurance and a way out.

01 Reassure first Say what’s safe before asking for a retry.
02 Warning, not error When the system fails, the user didn’t. Amber, not red.
03 No dead buttons Blocked buttons say what’s left, like 2 issues to fix.
